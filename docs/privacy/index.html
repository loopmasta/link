Link privacy policy

For the Link app for iPhone and this web site. Last updated: 23 November 2026.

In short: Link has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it sends nothing to the developer or to any server of ours. The app talks to the amateur radio network you connect it to. That network is run by its operators, who receive what a radio node necessarily sends: your node callsign, your voice while you hold the talk button, and the talkgroups you use.

Who is responsible

Stefan Beck, the developer of Link. Questions: github.com/loopmasta/link/issues.

What the app does with your data

DataWhat forWhere it goes
Microphone Your voice is picked up only while you hold the push-to-talk button, so it can be transmitted on the network. Sent encrypted to the network's reflector server, which passes it to the stations listening on that talkgroup. The app does not record or store it. What other stations or the operators do with audio on their network is up to them.
Node callsign You type it in Settings. The network needs it to log your node in and to show who is talking. Stored on the phone. Sent to the reflector server when you connect. The network shows it in its station lists and on its public dashboard.
Authentication key Proves to the reflector that the node is yours. Stored in the iOS Keychain on the phone. It is not sent itself: the app only sends a calculated answer to a challenge from the server. It is never shared with the developer.
Email address (optional) If you enter one in Settings, it goes into the node certificate request, which some networks need to contact you. Stored on the phone. Sent to the network operators in the certificate request, only if you entered it.
Node key and certificate The app creates a key pair for your node and asks the operators for a certificate. The private key stays in the iOS Keychain. The certificate request, which contains the callsign and the optional email, goes to the operators.
Talkgroups (your list with your own names, the selected one, the ones you also listen to) So the app is set up the way you left it, and so the reflector sends you the right audio. The list and the names stay on the phone. The selected talkgroup and the ones you listen to are sent to the reflector, and the operators' status feed publishes them as part of your node's record.
Software name and version The reflector asks every node which software it runs. Sent to the reflector and shown in the node's record.
IP address Any internet connection needs it. Seen by the servers the app connects to: the reflector and the status feed.
Settings and switches (host, screen stays on, level control, compression) So the app is set up the way you left it. Stored on the phone only.

The status feed

To show which stations are active, Link also reads the network operators' public status feed. This is a read-only connection with no login. It delivers who started and stopped talking, and the published records of the stations that are on your active list. Link only sends a random connection name and the names of the topics it wants, and those names contain the callsigns of the stations on your active list, which can include your own after you have transmitted. This connection is not encrypted, because the operators publish this information openly. The records of other stations are shown on screen only and are not stored.

When a station publishes a grid locator, its information sheet shows a small map of that place. The map is drawn by Apple Maps, so Apple receives the request for the map area (the station's place, not yours) under Apple's own privacy policy. The same applies if you tap "Open in Maps". Link does not ask for your location.

What the app does not do

  • No account, no sign-in with us, no advertising, no analytics, no tracking, no crash reporting of our own.
  • No location, contacts, photos or other personal data from your phone. The only permission it asks for is the microphone.
  • Nothing is sent to the developer.

If you have chosen in iOS to share analytics with app developers, Apple may provide the developer with anonymous crash and usage data. That is controlled by Apple and your iOS settings, not by the app.

Encryption

The connection to the reflector is encrypted (TLS), and the audio is encrypted as well. Link remembers each server's certificate authority the first time it connects and refuses to continue if it ever changes. The status feed is the exception described above.

The network operators

The reflector servers and the status feed belong to the operators of the network you connect to, not to the developer of Link. Their own privacy practices apply to what they receive. This policy covers only the Link app.

Storage and deleting

Everything the app keeps is on your phone. Deleting the app removes its settings. iOS may keep entries in the Keychain (the key, the certificate and the remembered certificate authority) after an app is deleted; only Link can read them. There is no account with us, so there is nothing for us to delete.

Children

Link is not aimed at children. Transmitting needs an amateur radio licence.

Third-party code

Link contains the open-source Opus audio codec and open-source Swift networking libraries. They run inside the app and do not send data anywhere on their own.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published on this page with a new date.

Link is an independent app and is not affiliated with the SvxLink project or any network operator.
